## Topic

How heterogeneous tourist populations choose destinations, and how destinations can use pricing, capacity, and branding to attract high-value tourists rather than drifting into a low-value mass-tourism equilibrium.

## Methods

- Population games (congestion games with heterogeneous agent types)
- Evolutionary dynamics (replicator dynamics over tourist strategy distributions)
- Mechanism design / Stackelberg games (destination as leader, tourist population as follower)

## Research question

[To be refined after literature review]

Preliminary: Under what conditions does type-segregation emerge naturally across destinations, and what instruments (price, capacity cap, branding) allow a destination to credibly commit to attracting high-value tourists without being invaded by low-value types?

## Core setup (from brainstorming)

- **Tourist types:** at least two -- high-value/congestion-averse (H) and low-value/congestion-tolerant (L). Payoff to type theta visiting destination j is u_theta(j, x) where x is the population state.
- **Key externality:** payoff depends on the *composition* of co-visitors, not just their count. H types are more sensitive to crowding by L types than by other H types.
- **Destinations:** choose instruments (price p, capacity k, branding b) knowing the equilibrium tourist response -- a Stackelberg/mechanism design layer on top of the population game.
- **Central tension:** attracting H types requires low congestion, but financial viability requires volume. A destination can fall into a "wrong equilibrium trap" where H types rationally avoid because L types dominate.

## Known adjacent literature (not yet reviewed)

- Sandholm (2010) -- population games and evolutionary dynamics (canonical)
- Rosenthal (1973) -- congestion games
- McFadden-style discrete choice models of destination selection (non-strategic baseline)
- Transport economics: Wardrop equilibria, congestion pricing
- Tourism carrying capacity literature (descriptive, not game-theoretic)

## Suspected gaps

- Heterogeneous-type congestion games where payoffs depend on visitor *composition* -- underworked even in the general population games literature
- Evolutionary dynamics applied to tourist type-frequency shifts over time
- Destination policy as mechanism design over the equilibrium map
- Type-segregation/mixing phase transitions and the wrong-equilibrium trap
