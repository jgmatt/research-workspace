## The economic problem to model

There are several IDENTICAL resorts (think 5 ski resorts), with no intrinsic differences in mountains, service, or quality. There is a heterogeneous population of tourists with different budgets / willingness-to-pay and possibly different preferences. We split them into (at least) two types: H ("rich", high budget) and L ("poor", low budget).

The CENTRAL question: starting from a baseline where all resorts set IDENTICAL policies (same prices), can the resorts increase their TOTAL profit by COORDINATING on DIFFERENTIATED policies that "split" the tourist base by type? E.g., one resort sets low prices and absorbs the poor tourists; another sets high prices and serves only the rich.

The KEY structural feature that should drive the result is an ASYMMETRIC congestion / peer externality:
- Rich (H) tourists dislike being around large numbers of poor (L) tourists.
- Poor (L) tourists do NOT mind being around rich tourists (the distaste is one-directional).
